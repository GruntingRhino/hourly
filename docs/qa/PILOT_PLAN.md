**Version:** 2.0  
**Last Updated:** 2026-06-29  
**Status:** Ready for founder review

## 1. Overview

This document defines the strategy for a controlled pilot launch of GoodHours. The pilot is a time-boxed, closely monitored deployment to a small, trusted group of real users. Its purpose is to:

1. Validate that the core workflows (student signup, hour tracking, org verification, school reporting) work correctly in the real world with real people
2. Identify usability gaps before broad adoption
3. Confirm system stability under real (if modest) load
4. Establish baseline metrics for measuring growth and engagement

A successful pilot is the condition for proceeding to general availability.

The pilot must prove:

1. Students can complete the full service journey (invite → register → check in → verify) without support.
2. Nonprofits can create opportunities, manage signups, and verify hours.
3. School admins can monitor student progress, export reports, and maintain accurate records.
4. The platform is stable under light real-world load.
5. Email delivery, hour verification, and audit trails work correctly in production.

## 2. Recommended Pilot Scope

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Duration | 4 weeks |
| Schools | 1 school (known, willing partner school) |
| Orgs | 2–3 nonprofits (at least 1 with recurring events) |
| Students | 20–50 active students (selected by school admin, not self-recruited) |
| Opportunities | 5–10 opportunities active during the pilot |

Start smaller than you think you need. A clean 20-student pilot is more valuable than a messy 200-student one.

**Why these numbers:**

**1 school:** Keeps data clean. One school admin as the primary point of contact. Limits blast radius if something goes wrong.

**2–3 orgs:** Enough to test multi-org workflows (org approval, multi-org browsing, cross-org hour verification) without overwhelming the founder's monitoring capacity.

**20–50 students:** Large enough to surface edge cases (capacity limits, waitlist promotion, duplicate signups) but small enough that the founder can review every submission manually if needed.

**4 weeks:** Long enough to observe a complete volunteer-hour lifecycle (sign up → attend → verify → report) at least once per student. Short enough to contain any data or UX problems before broad exposure.

### 2.1 Pilot Exclusions

The following features are **not** piloted in the first 4-week run:

- Live Stripe payments (billing not activated during pilot)
- Canvas or Google Classroom live OAuth (mock mode only during pilot)
- Self-service school signup (school admin account created manually)

## 3. Pilot Onboarding Steps

### Week -2: Platform Readiness (Two Weeks Before Pilot Start)

**Platform:**
- [ ] Production deployment on HTTPS domain. All items in `docs/qa/PRODUCTION_CHECKLIST.md` complete.
- [ ] Real Resend API key configured and sending domain verified. Test email sent and confirmed working.
- [ ] Stripe billing deferred to post-pilot (do not activate live Stripe during pilot unless explicitly planned).
- [ ] Database backups verified: automated daily backup enabled, restore tested from a snapshot.
- [ ] Error tracking enabled (Sentry or equivalent). Test alert triggered and confirmed received.
- [ ] Health monitoring configured: uptime alert to founder's phone.
- [ ] All seed/test accounts removed from the production database or marked `isTestAccount=true` and excluded from reports.
- [ ] Support contact (`support@goodhours.app` or equivalent) confirmed live and monitored.

**School onboarding:**
- [ ] School admin account created manually.
- [ ] School required service hour goal configured.
- [ ] School email domain set in `APPROVED_SCHOOL_DOMAINS`.
- [ ] School admin has approved all pilot orgs.
- [ ] Cohort/group created for the pilot students.

**Org onboarding:**
- [ ] Org accounts created and email-verified for each pilot org.
- [ ] 15-minute screenshare walkthrough completed with each org admin.
- [ ] Each org has created at least one real upcoming opportunity with accurate date, time, location, and capacity.
- [ ] Orgs approved by the school admin in the platform.

### Week -1: Student Onboarding (One Week Before Pilot Start)

- [ ] Student invitation codes or direct signup links generated by school admin.
- [ ] Invitations sent to 20–50 pilot students through the school's existing channel (email list, LMS, etc.).
- [ ] 48-hour onboarding window opened. Students notified of deadline.
- [ ] After 48 hours: verify that all expected student accounts exist with email verification complete. Follow up manually with any stragglers.
- [ ] One-page student orientation guide distributed: "How to find and sign up for an opportunity."
- [ ] End-to-end dry run completed by the founder or team member: full student journey from signup through verification submission.
- [ ] End-to-end dry run completed: full org journey from opportunity creation through hour approval.
- [ ] Support contact communicated clearly to all participants.

## 4. Success Metrics

Track these weekly. Report at the end of each week and cumulatively at pilot close.

### 4.1 Activation Metrics

| Metric | Definition | Target |
|--------|-----------|--------|
| **Account signup completion rate** | % of invited students who completed signup (email verified) | ≥ 80% |
| **First opportunity sign-up rate** | % of verified students who signed up for at least one opportunity | ≥ 60% |
| **Org opportunity creation** | % of pilot orgs that published at least one real opportunity | 100% |

### 4.2 Core Workflow Metrics

| Metric | Green | Yellow | Red |
|--------|-------|--------|-----|
| Invitation acceptance rate | ≥ 70% | 40–70% | < 40% |
| Opportunity signup rate | ≥ 60% of students sign up for ≥1 event | 30–60% | < 30% |
| Check-in completion | ≥ 80% of signups check in | 60–80% | < 60% |
| Hour verification rate (within 72h) | ≥ 90% | 70–90% | < 70% |
| Org verification response time (median) | ≤ 24h | 24–48h | > 48h |

### 4.3 Quality Metrics

| Metric | Green | Yellow | Red |
|--------|-------|--------|-----|
| Server 5xx error rate | < 0.5% | 0.5–2% | > 2% |
| Hour submission error rate | < 2% | 2–5% | > 5% |
| Support ticket volume (per week) | < 2 per 10 students | 2–5 per 10 | > 5 per 10 |
| Critical (P0) bugs | 0 | — | Any |
| Email delivery rate | ≥ 98% | 95–98% | < 95% |
| Audit trail integrity | 100% spot-checks pass | — | Any failure |

## 5. Weekly Monitoring Checklist

### Every Day During Pilot

- [ ] Review error tracking dashboard (look for 500s, auth errors, data errors)
- [ ] Check failed email queue in Resend dashboard
- [ ] Spot-check hour totals for 2–3 random students (API vs. dashboard)

### Every Week

- [ ] Export CSV for the school and verify row counts match dashboard
- [ ] Check audit log for any OVERRIDE entries (unexpected hour corrections)
- [ ] Review all rejected hour submissions and their reasons
- [ ] Run data integrity check: `GET /api/reports/student` total vs. session sum
- [ ] Review Stripe subscription status if billing is active

## 6. Escalation Triggers

The following events require immediate response. Contact the technical owner within 30 minutes:

| Trigger | Response |
|---|---|
| Any student can access another student's verified hours | **P0** — take down service, investigate IDOR |
| Hour totals don't reconcile | **P0** — freeze all verification, audit database |
| Unauthorized admin access | **P0** — revoke all tokens, investigate |
| Email verification links stop working | **P1** — all new signups blocked, investigate email config |
| Student reports hours disappearing | **P1** — check audit log, do not write new records until root cause found |
| > 10% of API requests returning 500 | **P1** — roll back most recent deploy if applicable |
| Database backup failing | **P2** — fix backup before any further data creation |

## 7. Pilot Week-by-Week Plan

### Week 1 — Onboarding
**Goal:** All pilot participants have accounts and at least one signup.  
**Check:** ≥ 70% of invited students have accepted invitations.  
**Risk:** Email delivery issues blocking invitation acceptance.  
**Mitigation:** Have dev bypass endpoint ready (`POST /api/auth/dev/bypass-email-verification`) if production email is misconfigured.

### Week 2 — First Events
**Goal:** First round of service events occurs. Students check in and out. Orgs verify hours.  
**Check:** At least 80% of students who signed up successfully checked out.  
**Risk:** Students not receiving check-in reminders (email config).  
**Mitigation:** Orgs manually remind students via the messaging feature.

### Week 3 — Verification Cycle
**Goal:** Verified hours appear on student dashboards. School admin reviews reports.  
**Check:** CSV export matches dashboard totals for all students.  
**Risk:** Hours in "pending" state — org hasn't verified.  
**Mitigation:** School admin sends a reminder to orgs via messages.

### Week 4 — Evaluation and Decision
**Goal:** Collect feedback, analyze metrics, decide on full launch.  
**Check:** Run all 5 success metrics (table above). Count support tickets. Read feedback.  
**Decision criteria:** If all metrics are Green or Yellow and no Critical/P0 issues occurred, proceed to full launch planning.

---

## 8. Post-Pilot Evaluation Criteria for Full Launch

All of the following must be true before unrestricted launch:

- [ ] Zero Critical or P0 incidents during the pilot
- [ ] Hour total reconciliation: 100% accuracy across all pilot students
- [ ] Invitation acceptance rate ≥ 60%
- [ ] Hour verification rate ≥ 85%
- [ ] Support ticket volume below 3 per week per 20 students
- [ ] Email delivery rate ≥ 97%
- [ ] Audit logs complete for all verification events (no missing entries)
- [ ] School admin can export accurate CSV without assistance
- [ ] At least one nonprofit confirms they would recommend the platform

---

## 9. Data Retention and Privacy During Pilot

- All pilot data is real student data subject to FERPA protections
- Do not share individual student records with unauthorized parties
- If a pilot participant withdraws, their data can be deactivated but must be retained per school policy
- Hourly data exports should be stored securely, not in shared drives without access controls
- Review data retention policy with a legal professional before launch
